# Northwind Robotics — Series A

**SYNTHETIC SAMPLE DOCUMENT. Northwind Robotics is a fictional company.**

Raising $18M Series A · Seattle, WA · Founded 2023

---

## 1. The problem

Third-party logistics warehouses run at 68% of theoretical throughput. The
binding constraint is not shelving or software — it is labour. A 400,000 sq ft
facility needs 240 pickers per shift, turnover runs 90% annually, and peak
season demands a 3x headcount ramp that simply is not available in most
metros.

Existing automation does not fix this. Fixed goods-to-person systems cost
$40–80M, take 18 months to install, and lock a floor plan in place for a decade.
Operators who lease their buildings on 5-year terms cannot underwrite that.

## 2. What we built

Northwind makes an autonomous mobile picking unit that works inside existing
racking. No floor modification, no ceiling rail, no facility shutdown. A crew of
two installs a 40-unit fleet in nine days.

- Vision stack handles 94.2% of SKUs in a general-merchandise catalogue without
  per-item training
- Units navigate alongside human pickers rather than in a caged zone
- Fleet manager integrates with Manhattan, Blue Yonder and Körber WMS out of the box

Eight of eleven facilities began as a 90-day paid pilot. Seven converted. The
one that did not was a cold-storage site at −18°C, which we now disqualify at
the top of the funnel.

**Revenue model.** Robots-as-a-service at $3,400 per unit per month, 24-month
minimum term. No hardware sale, no capex line for the customer.

## 4. Unit economics

- Bill of materials: $11,800 per unit, down from $19,400 at first article
- Landed cost including integration labour: $14,200
- Payback on a deployed unit: 4.2 months
- Contribution margin per unit per month: $1,760
- Expected service life: 5 years, one refurbishment at month 30 ($2,100)

Gross margin is 52% today and rises to 61% at 2,000 cumulative units, which is
when our contract manufacturer's tooling amortisation ends.

## 5. Market

- 5,400 warehouses in North America above 250,000 sq ft
- Average addressable fleet: 85 units
- Serviceable obtainable market at current pricing: $18.7B annually
- We are targeting 3% penetration by 2030

## 6. Competition

**Locus Robotics, 6 River Systems** — established, but both are cart-following
systems that still require a human to pick the item. We remove the human from
the pick itself, which is where 70% of the labour cost sits.

**Symbotic, AutoStore** — fixed infrastructure. Different buyer (capex committee,
not operations), different sales cycle (14 months vs our 3), different customer
(owner-occupier, not leaseholder).

**Amazon Robotics** — not sold externally. Our risk is not that they compete,
it is that they set customer expectations for a price point we cannot match at
our volume.

**Our moat.** 2.3M labelled grasp attempts from live facilities. A new entrant
starting today needs roughly 18 months of deployed operation to match the SKU
generalisation rate, and cannot get that data without customers who will only
sign for a system that already works.

- 12% — general and administrative

## 10. Risks we would flag ourselves

- **Customer concentration.** Our largest facility is 31% of ARR. The contract
  runs to March 2028 but there is no exclusivity.
- **Single-source components.** The depth sensor has one qualified supplier. A
  second source is in qualification, expected complete Q4 2026.
- **Tariff exposure.** 61% of BOM value originates in China. A 25% tariff moves
  gross margin from 52% to 43%.
- **Labour politics.** Two of our eleven sites are unionised. Both agreements
  are silent on automation; the 2027 renegotiation is a live risk.
